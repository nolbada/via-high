다음 글의 밑줄 친 부분 중, 어법상 <u>틀린</u> 것은?

문제 본문 다시 보기
The very fact that all of our experiences “take place” in a physical space makes the space inseparable from ①<u>what</u> defines it, from the event that took place in it. The tree and the ground beneath are inseparable from what took place ②<u>there</u>. This intertwining of space, place, and experience challenges the idea of place as passive backdrop. If place and space are central to the very heart of experience, we must ③<u>accept</u> the possibility that the spatial framework is the medium of all experiences—actions, thoughts, memories, and feelings of every place—not merely a background or context exterior to experience (as commonly supposed). Belgian literary critic Georges Poulet made the point elegantly, ④<u>writing</u> of the space created in Marcel Proust’s great novel In Search of Lost Time: “Beings surround themselves with the places ⑤<u>when</u> they find themselves, the way one wraps oneself up in a garment that is at one and the same time a disguise and a characterization. Without places, beings would be only abstractions.” * intertwine: 뒤얽히다 ** garment: 의복
정답: ⑤

보기와 우리말 뜻

① what (~하는 것 → from의 목적어가 되는 명사절이라 맞아요.)

② there (거기에서 → 장소 부사라 맞아요.)

③ accept (받아들이다 → must 뒤 동사원형이라 맞아요.)

④ writing (쓰면서 → 앞 문장에 덧붙이는 분사구문이라 맞아요.)

⑤ when (~할 때 → 장소 the places 뒤라서 where가 되어야 해요.)

답의 근거 (본문)

The tree and the ground beneath are inseparable from what took place (나무와 그 아래 땅은 거기서 일어난 일과 떼어 놓을 수 없어요)

Beings surround themselves with the places when they find themselves (존재들은 자신이 놓인 장소로 스스로를 감싼다 (장소 the places를 꾸미므로 when이 아니라 where가 와야 해요))

쉬운 풀이

'the places(장소)' 뒤에는 '그 장소에서(where) 그들이 자신을 발견하는'이라는 설명이 와요. 장소를 꾸며 줄 때는 when(때)이 아니라 where(곳)를 써야 해요.

다른 보기는 왜 아닐까요?

① what은 'from' 뒤 명사절이라 맞아요.

② there는 부사로 맞아요.

③ must 뒤 동사원형 accept가 맞아요.

④ writing은 분사구문으로 맞아요.

우리의 모든 경험은 물리적 공간에서 일어나서, 공간은 그곳에서 벌어진 일과 떼어 놓을 수 없어요. 그래서 장소를 그저 수동적인 배경으로 보는 생각에 의문이 생겨요. 공간은 행동, 생각, 기억, 감정 같은 모든 경험의 매개체예요. 풀레는 존재들이 장소로 자신을 감싸고, 장소가 없으면 추상적인 것에 불과하다고 말했어요.

다음 글의 밑줄 친 부분 중, 문맥상 낱말의 쓰임이 적절하지 않은 것은?

문제 본문 다시 보기
The very fact that all of our experiences “take place” in a physical space makes the space inherently ① <u>linked</u> to what defines it, from the event that took place in it. The tree and the ground beneath are inseparable from what took place there. This intertwining of space, place, and experience ② <u>questions</u> the idea of place as passive backdrop. If place and space are central to the very heart of experience, we must accept the possibility that the spatial framework is the medium of all experiences — actions, thoughts, memories, and feelings of every place — not merely a background or context ③ <u>exterior</u> to experience (as commonly supposed). Belgian literary critic Georges Poulet made the point elegantly, writing of the space created in Marcel Proust’s great novel In Search of Lost Time: “Beings surround themselves with the places where they are ④ <u>situated</u>, the way one wraps oneself up in a garment that is at one and the same time a disguise and a characterization. Thanks to places, beings can exist as ⑤ <u>abstractions</u>.” [from The Feeling of Space by Christopher Bardt]
정답: ⑤

보기와 우리말 뜻

① linked (연결된 → inseparable(떼어 놓을 수 없는)과 비슷해서 맞아요.)

② questions (의문을 제기하다 → challenges와 비슷해서 맞아요.)

③ exterior (바깥쪽의 → 경험 바깥의 배경이라는 뜻으로 맞아요.)

④ situated (놓여 있는 → where they are situated로 맞아요.)

⑤ abstractions (추상적인 것 → 장소 덕분에 '추상'으로 존재한다는 말은 글과 반대예요.)

답의 근거 (본문)

Thanks to places, beings can exist as (장소 덕분에 존재들은 ~로서 존재할 수 있어요)

The tree and the ground beneath are inseparable from what took place there. (나무와 그 아래 땅은 거기서 일어난 일과 떼어 놓을 수 없어요.)

the way one wraps oneself up in a garment that is at one and the same time a disguise and a characterization. (마치 변장이자 특징 표현인 옷으로 몸을 감싸듯이요.)

쉬운 풀이

이 글은 장소와 존재가 떼어 놓을 수 없다고 말해요. 그러니 '장소가 없다면 존재는 추상적일 뿐'이어야 해요. 'Thanks to places, beings can exist as abstractions(장소 덕분에 추상적인 것으로 존재할 수 있다)'는 반대 뜻이라 ⑤가 어색해요.

다른 보기는 왜 아닐까요?

① linked는 맞아요.

② questions는 맞아요.

③ exterior는 맞아요.

④ situated는 맞아요.

문제 본문 다시 보기
All sound begins with some kind of motion, something that disturbs molecules in air, water, steel, wood, or other medium. Isaac Newton noted that light waves are colorless; the perception of color only occurs in the brain of a living organism. The same is true of sound. Thus perception of sound begins at our eardrums—before it hits our eardrums, it is nothing more than the disturbance of molecules. We can measure that disturbance with gauges and instruments, but until it hits a brain, it is not sound. You may have heard the old philosophical riddle, “If a tree falls in the forest and no one is there to hear it, does it make a sound?” No, because sound is a psychological concept, a product of brains.
