Some people are skeptical about the idea of global citizenship, but for most of human history, people didn't think of themselves as citizens of nation-states. ⑤ (C)가 맨 앞에 올 수 없어요. 주어진 글의 '국민국가' 이야기는 (B)의 After all(어쨌든)로 바로 이어져야 해요.

문제 본문 다시 보기
Among unsatisfied professionals, there’s a repeating theme—the belief that someday (a mysterious day in the future) things will miraculously change and they’ll stumble into the perfect job or career. At the same time, they (typically) do little to meaningfully alter their current situation. They don’t like their job, so what do they do? They head to job boards and search for roles with a similar job title, hoping that the universe will sprinkle a bit of magic on their career if they just hit the refresh button in a different department or company. Efforts like this make professionals feel like they are doing something, when really they’re just setting themselves up to take a new role and quickly become just as disenchanted as they were before. If you keep playing the same game, you’ll win the same prizes. * stumble into: ~을 우연히 마주치다 ** disenchanted: 환상이 깨진

완벽한 직업을 찾기 위해서는 구직 사이트를 적극 활용해야 한다.

직업에 불만족한다면 표면적인 변화가 아닌 접근 방식을 완전히 바꿔야 한다.

미래에 대한 막연한 기대보다는 현재 주어진 업무에 최선을 다하는 것이 중요하다.

Sociologists argue that our perception and usage of time are not just basic properties of nature, like a clock ticking away in the background, nor are they simply psychological phenomena of individual experience. Instead, how we perceive and understand time is to a large degree shaped by tradition and cultural norms. Eviatar Zerubavel’s work, The Seven Day Circle: The History and Meaning of the Week, demonstrates how the week, a fundamental unit of time, is a social invention that varies across cultures, influencing how people structure their activities and rhythms of life. Why do we consider a nine-to-five job routine? Why don’t many of us work on the weekends? Why do we usually pay our bills at the end of the month? Why is the retirement age sixty-five? These numbers and patterns aren’t really dictated by nature or any essential human characteristic, but are rather social creations that we internalize and come to accept as “normal.” [from Irrational Together by Adam S. Hayes]

In recent years, the number of illegal actions in particular at the corporate level has grown, or at least the number of cases that came to light, increased. Such misconduct has often only been exposed because brave informants have risked a great deal in order to put an end to it. Hence, it is no surprise that whistleblower protection has come into focus. If violations of law and order go undetected and unpunished for years because employees of a company fear to report illegal activities for fear of reprisals or consequences, it can have several negative effects. It can harm the company itself, but also third-parties, the economy in general, the general public, and even the rule of law could be affected. Protecting those who would assist in reducing unlawful or even criminal behaviour has two main functions: Protecting the individual’s actions as well as protecting the public good. A well-balanced whistleblower protection may have positive effects on both of these. *whistleblower: (기업 비리 등의) 내부 고발자 **reprisal: 보복

② 국제법 제정 이야기는 없어요.

보기와 우리말 뜻

ⓐ that / ⓑ are / ⓒ gained → ⓐ가 틀려요 (that 대신 가짜 주어 it이 맞아요)

ⓐ that / ⓑ is / ⓒ are gained → ⓐ that, ⓑ is(~이다, 단수), ⓒ are gained(얻어진다) 모두 틀려요

ⓐ it / ⓑ are / ⓒ gained → ⓐ it (가짜 주어), ⓑ are (~이다, 복수 rats or mice에 맞춤), ⓒ gained (~에서 얻어진, findings를 꾸밈)

ⓐ it / ⓑ is / ⓒ gained → ⓑ가 틀려요 (is 대신 복수형 are)

ⓐ it / ⓑ are / ⓒ are gained → ⓒ가 틀려요 (are gained 대신 gained)

답의 근거 (본문)

is not at all clear how relevant those findings will be to human aging (그 발견이 사람의 노화에 얼마나 관련 있을지는 전혀 분명하지 않다)

다른 보기는 왜 아닐까요?

① ⓐ에 that을 쓰면 뒤의 how ~ aging 덩어리가 붙을 곳이 없어요. 진짜 주어(how ~ aging)를 뒤로 보내고 앞자리에 두는 가짜 주어는 it이라서 틀려요.

④ ⓑ에서 which의 앞말 rats or mice가 복수라서 is는 틀려요. are가 맞아요.

⑤ ⓒ에서 are gained를 쓰면 한 문장에 동사가 둘이 되어 틀려요. gained가 맞아요.
