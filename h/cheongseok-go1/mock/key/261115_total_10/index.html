문제 본문 다시 보기
Unfortunately, sometimes the idea of a goal is disconnected from the daily reality of what it will take to achieve it. We fall in love with the idea of a goal, pushing out of mind how different our day-to-day will need to be for us to make it happen. In fact, research even suggests that positive fantasies about our future can lead us to put less effort into our goals because they __________. We romanticize a goal — falling in love with the idea of a change. In the process, we gloss over the intricacies and challenges of integrating a complex new behavior into our life. As one study nicely summed it up, fantasizing about a positive future “seduces a person to mentally enjoy the desired future in the here and now” — and because we don’t reflect on why our fantasy is not yet our future, “a necessity to act is not induced.”
정답: ②

보기와 우리말 뜻

① make us more conscious of the practical difficulties involved in achieving success. → 성공에 따르는 현실적 어려움을 더 의식하게 만든다

As one study nicely summed it up, fantasizing about a positive future “seduces a person to mentally enjoy the desired future in the here and now” — and because we don’t reflect on why our fantasy is not yet our future, “a necessity to act is not induced.” (한 연구가 잘 요약했듯이, 긍정적인 미래를 공상하는 것은 '사람을 꾀어 바라는 미래를 지금 여기에서 머릿속으로 즐기게' 만든다. 그리고 우리는 왜 환상이 아직 미래가 되지 않았는지 돌아보지 않기 때문에 '행동할 필요성이 생겨나지 않는다.')

즉, 이미 이룬 것 같은 기분이 들어서 노력을 덜 해요.

그래서 ②가 정답이에요.

다른 보기는 왜 아닐까요?

① 어려움을 의식하게 하면 노력이 늘어야 하니 반대예요.

③ 현실적인 계획을 세우게 한다는 건 글과 반대예요.

④ 결심을 강하게 한다면 노력이 줄 이유가 없어요.

⑤ 차이를 알아차리는 건 글에서 '하지 않는다'고 한 행동이에요.

Since hydra and the immortal jellyfish don’t exhibit obvious signs of aging, they are often called biologically immortal. This doesn’t mean they don’t die. Nor does it even mean that they cannot die of biological causes. But, unlike most every animal, their likelihood of dying does not increase with age. Species such as hydra and the immortal jellyfish excite gerontologists because they may provide clues about how to defeat the aging process. But their property of being able to regenerate entire body parts, or even a whole organism, makes them more similar to trees than to us. Although we may learn some fascinating things about their lack of apparent aging, it is not at all clear how relevant those findings will be to human aging. Sometimes biology is universal, especially if it relates to fundamental mechanisms. But in other cases, even discoveries in rats or mice, which are mammals and biologically much closer to us, are difficult to translate into humans. It may be a very long time before any findings gained from hydra or jellyfish are useful to us.

문제 본문 다시 보기
Subsidies, taxes, and regulations can all lead to power plants that pollute less by switching away from coal. In all cases, profits can be higher from making the transition, even taking the related costs into account. (A) But the “expansion” of their opportunity set through subsidies has to be matched by the contraction of the opportunity set of others. The electricity companies’ “freedom” is increased at the expense of the ordinary individual, who must suffer from increased taxation to pay for the subsidy. (B) Moreover, from the perspective of pure efficiency, the subsidy distorts the economy because the electricity company is not facing the full costs of what it does, even if it is successful in limiting emissions. Indeed, electricity prices may end up lower than they should be and electricity consumption higher, which will worsen global warming, even though the emissions per unit of electricity have been reduced. (C) The power plants prefer subsidies over regulations or taxes. The reason is obvious. They prefer to receive money from the government rather than pay the costs themselves.
정답: ④

보기와 우리말 뜻

① (A) - (C) - (B) → (A)의 their(그들의)가 가리킬 발전소의 선호가 아직 안 나왔어요.

② (B) - (A) - (C) → Moreover(게다가)로 시작하는 (B)가 맨 앞일 수 없어요.

③ (B) - (C) - (A) → (B)가 맨 앞이라 틀려요.

④ (C) - (A) - (B) → 보조금 선호 → 그 대가(시민의 부담) → 게다가 경제 왜곡 순서라 맞아요.

⑤ (C) - (B) - (A) → But(하지만)으로 시작하는 (A)가 Moreover(게다가) 뒤에 오면 흐름이 거꾸로예요.

답의 근거 (본문)

The power plants prefer subsidies over regulations or taxes. (발전소들은 규제나 세금보다 보조금을 더 좋아한다.)

But the “expansion” of their opportunity set through subsidies has to be matched by the contraction of the opportunity set of others. (하지만 보조금을 통한 그들의 기회 범위 '확대'는 다른 사람들의 기회 범위 축소와 짝을 이룰 수밖에 없다.)

Moreover, from the perspective of pure efficiency, the subsidy distorts the economy because the electricity company is not facing the full costs of what it does, even if it is successful in limiting emissions. (게다가 순수한 효율성의 관점에서 보면, 보조금은 경제를 왜곡한다. 전력 회사가 배출을 줄이는 데 성공하더라도 자기가 하는 일의 전체 비용을 마주하지 않기 때문이다.)

쉬운 풀이

주어진 글은 보조금·세금·규제 모두 이익을 낼 수 있다는 내용이에요.

(C)는 그중 발전소가 보조금을 더 좋아하는 이유를 말해요.

(A)는 But(하지만)으로 그 보조금의 대가, 즉 시민의 손해를 말해요.

(B)는 Moreover(게다가)로 두 번째 문제인 경제 왜곡을 더해요.

그래서 (C)-(A)-(B), ④가 정답이에요.

다른 보기는 왜 아닐까요?

① (A)의 '그들의 기회 확대'가 무엇인지 (C)가 먼저 설명해야 해요.

② Moreover(게다가)는 첫 문제 다음에 와야 해요.

③ (B)가 맨 앞에 올 수 없어요.

⑤ 첫 번째 문제(A)보다 '게다가'(B)가 먼저 오면 안 돼요.

시험 포인트

But(하지만) → Moreover(게다가)처럼 문제점을 차례로 더하는 연결어의 순서를 따라가면 쉽게 풀려요.

문제 본문 다시 보기
Subsidies, taxes, and regulations can all lead to power plants that pollute less by switching away from coal. In all cases, profits can be higher from making the transition, even taking the related costs into account. The power plants prefer subsidies over regulations or taxes. The reason is obvious. They prefer to receive money from the government rather than pay the costs themselves. But the “expansion” of their opportunity set through subsidies has to be matched by the contraction of the opportunity set of others. ① ⓐ increased → 늘어난. 늘어난 세금으로 고통받는다는 뜻으로 맞아요.

② ⓑ distorts → 왜곡하다. 보조금이 경제를 왜곡한다는 뜻으로 맞아요.

③ ⓒ full → 전체의. 전체 비용을 마주하지 않는다는 뜻으로 맞아요.

④ ⓓ higher → 더 높은. 비용을 다 안 내서 가격은 '더 낮아져야' 하니 틀려요.

회사는 보조금 덕분에 전체 비용을 내지 않아요.

그러면 전기 가격은 마땅한 수준보다 '낮아져요(lower)'.

가격이 싸니 소비가 '많아지고(higher)' 온난화가 악화돼요.

그래서 가격 자리의 higher(더 높은)는 틀리고 ④가 정답이에요.

① increased taxation(늘어난 세금)은 맞아요.

② distorts the economy(경제를 왜곡하다)는 맞아요.

③ full costs(전체 비용)는 맞아요.

⑤ worsen global warming(온난화를 악화시키다)은 맞아요.

Few people may view the Star Trek universe through an economic lens, but doing so provides a window into a world that might soon be upon us. All of classical economics is based on the notion that there isn’t usually enough of anything to give everyone everything they want or need. Because of that, we use markets and pricing to allocate those goods, services, and resources to where they might result in the highest benefit. In Star Trek, however, there isn’t much scarcity.
