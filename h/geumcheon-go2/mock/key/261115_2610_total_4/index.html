문제 본문 다시 보기
A person who lives eighty years lives some twenty-nine thousand days, and the overwhelming majority of the behaviour packed into them runs on settings laid down, most of them, decades earlier and rarely inspected since. This need not be a hopeless thought. In one light it is a triumph of engineering. If you had to consciously decide every footstep and every fork-grip and every word, you would never make it out of bed; the deliberate mind is far too slow and too costly to run a whole life in real time, and any creature that tried would be outcompeted by a creature that didn’t. The automation is precisely what frees the captain to come up on deck for the few problems that actually require him. The default is not a flaw in the human design. It is arguably the central feature, the thing that makes a complex life affordable at all.

보기와 우리말 뜻

⑤ The human brain is inherently imperfect and requires deliberate corrections to function properly. (인간의 뇌는 본래 불완전해 의식적인 교정이 필요하다.) → 결함이 아니라는 말과 반대예요.

Like all historians, social historians draw from a variety of primary sources. Social history often emphasizes ordinary people rather than elites. Yet, in most places and times, nonelites lacked access to education and, thus, did not produce substantial amounts of documentation. Even when they did produce documentation, these materials rarely ended up in archives where they could be preserved and made available to researchers. Because of such biases in the historical record, social historians have to be very creative in their work. Like other historians, social historians analyze textual sources produced by government officials, employers, and other elites. Yet rather than simply take such sources at face value, they read them “against the grain”.
