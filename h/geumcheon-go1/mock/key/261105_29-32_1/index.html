문제 본문 다시 보기
Since hydra and the immortal jellyfish don’t exhibit obvious signs of aging, they are often called biologically immortal. This doesn’t mean they don’t die. Nor does it even mean that they cannot die of biological causes. But, unlike most every animal, their likelihood of dying does not increase with age. Species such as hydra and the immortal jellyfish excite gerontologists because they may provide clues about how to defeat the aging process. But their property of being able to regenerate entire body parts, or even a whole organism, makes them more similar to trees than to us. Although we may learn some fascinating things about their lack of apparent aging, it is not at all clear how relevant those findings will be to human aging. Sometimes biology is universal, especially if it relates to fundamental mechanisms. But in other cases, even discoveries in rats or mice, which are mammals and biologically much closer to us, are difficult to translate into humans. It may be a very long time before any findings gained from hydra or jellyfish are useful to us. * gerontologist: 노화학자

쉬운 풀이

다른 보기는 왜 아닐까요?

① 불멸의 정의를 설명하는 글이 아니에요. 처음에 잠깐 나오는 소개일 뿐이에요.

② 포유류와 바다 동물의 노화 속도를 비교하는 내용은 없어요.

③ 재생 능력은 한계를 말하려고 잠깐 나온 부분이고, 그 과정을 설명하지는 않아요.

⑤ 새로운 노화 극복 방법의 필요성을 주장하는 글이 아니에요. 오히려 연구 결과 적용이 어렵다고 해요.
