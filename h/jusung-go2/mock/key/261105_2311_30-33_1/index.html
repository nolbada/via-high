문제 본문 다시 보기
A coincidence that appears statistically impossible strikes us as irrational, leading some to label it a miracle. However, as Montaigne observed, “the origin of a miracle lies in our ignorance—specifically, in the limits of our knowledge of nature, not in nature itself.” Glorious miracles have been later on discovered to be obedience to the laws of nature or a technological development that was not widely known at the time. As the German poet, Goethe, phrased it: “Things that are mysterious are not yet miracles.” The miracle assumes the intervention of a “higher power” in its occurrence that is beyond human capability to grasp. Yet there are methodical and simple ways to “cause a miracle” without divine revelation and inspiration. Instead of checking it out, investigating and finding the source of the event, we define it as a miracle. A miracle, then, becomes an excuse for those unwilling to think critically.

문제 본문 다시 보기
Information encountered after an event can influence subsequent remembering. External information can easily integrate into a witness’s memory, especially if the event was poorly encoded or the memory is from a distant event, in which case time and forgetting have degraded the original memory. With reduced information available in memory with which to confirm the validity of post-event misinformation, it is less likely that this new information will be rejected. Instead, especially when it fits the witness’s current thinking and can be used to create a story that makes sense to him or her, it may be integrated as part of the original experience. This process can be explicit (i.e., the witness knows it is happening), but it is often unconscious. That is, the witness might find himself or herself thinking about the event differently without awareness. Over time, the witness may not even know the source of information that led to the (new) memory. Sources of misinformation in forensic contexts can be encountered anywhere, from discussions with other witnesses to social media searches to multiple interviews with investigators or other legal professionals, and even in court.

문제 본문 다시 보기
Information encountered after an event can influence subsequent remembering. External information can easily integrate into a witness’s memory, especially if the event was poorly encoded or the memory is from a distant event, in which case time and forgetting have degraded the original memory. With reduced information available in memory with which to confirm the validity of post-event misinformation, it is less likely that this new information will be rejected. Instead, especially when it fits the witness’s current thinking and can be used to create a story that makes sense to him or her, it may be integrated as part of the original experience. This process can be explicit (i.e., the witness knows it is happening), but it is often unconscious. That is, the witness might find himself or herself thinking about the event differently without awareness. Over time, the witness may not even know the source of information that led to the (new) memory. Sources of misinformation in forensic contexts can be encountered anywhere, from discussions with other witnesses to social media searches to multiple interviews with investigators or other legal professionals, and even in court. * forensic: 법정의

문제 본문 다시 보기
Correlations are powerful because the insights they offer are relatively clear. These insights are often covered up when we bring causality back into the picture. For instance, a used-car dealer supplied data to statisticians to predict which of the vehicles available for purchase at an auction were likely to have problems. A correlation analysis showed that orange-colored cars were far less likely to have defects. Even as we read this, we already think about why it might be so: Are orange-colored car owners likely to be car enthusiasts and take better care of their vehicles? Or, is it because orange-colored cars are more noticeable on the road and therefore less likely to be in accidents, so they’re in better condition when resold? Quickly we are caught in a web of competing causal hypotheses. But our attempts to illuminate things this way only make them cloudier. Correlations exist; we can show them mathematically. We can’t easily do the same for causal links. So we would do well to hold off from trying to explain the reason behind the correlations.

다음 글의 내용과 일치하지 않는 것은?

문제 본문 다시 보기
Correlations are powerful because the insights they offer are relatively clear. These insights are often covered up when we bring causality back into the picture. For instance, a used-car dealer supplied data to statisticians to predict which of the vehicles available for purchase at an auction were likely to have problems. A correlation analysis showed that orange-colored cars were far less likely to have defects. Even as we read this, we already think about why it might be so: Are orange-colored car owners likely to be car enthusiasts and take better care of their vehicles? Or, is it because orange-colored cars are more noticeable on the road and therefore less likely to be in accidents, so they’re in better condition when resold? Quickly we are caught in a web of competing causal hypotheses. But our attempts to illuminate things this way only make them cloudier. Correlations exist; we can show them mathematically. We can’t easily do the same for causal links. So we would do well to hold off from trying to explain the reason behind the correlations.
정답: ④

보기와 우리말 뜻

상관관계는 비교적 명확한 통찰력을 제공한다. (the insights they offer are relatively clear)

인과관계를 도입하면 경쟁적 인과 가설에 빠지기 쉽다. (a web of competing causal hypotheses)

상관관계의 존재는 수학적으로 보여줄 수 있다. (we can show them mathematically)

인과관계는 수학적으로 증명이 불가능하다. (We can't easily do the same for causal links)

답의 근거 (본문)

Correlations exist; we can show them mathematically. (상관관계는 존재하고, 우리는 그것을 수학적으로 보여줄 수 있어요.)

We can’t easily do the same for causal links. (인과적 연결에 대해서는 같은 일을 쉽게 할 수 없어요.)

쉬운 풀이

본문은 인과관계를 수학적으로 보여주는 것이 '쉽지 않다(can't easily)'고 했지, '불가능하다'고 하지는 않았어요. 그래서 ④가 본문과 일치하지 않아요.

③ 상관관계는 수학적으로 보여줄 수 있다고 했어요.

⑤ 마지막 문장에서 상관관계 뒤의 이유 설명은 미루는 게 좋다고 했어요.
