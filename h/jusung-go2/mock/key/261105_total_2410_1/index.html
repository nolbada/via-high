To determine the mass of my bowling ball, I might put it onto a balance and compare it with a known mass, such as a number of metal cubes each weighing 1, 10, or 100 grams. Things get much more complicated if I want to know the mass of a distant star. How do I measure it? We can roughly say that measuring the mass of a star involves various theories. If we want to measure the mass of a binary star, we first determine a center of mass between the two stars, then their distance from that center which we can then use, together with a value for the period and a certain instance of Kepler’s Third Law, to calculate the mass. In other words, in order to “measure” the star mass, we measure other quantities and use those values, together with certain equations, to calculate the mass. Measurement is not a simple and unmediated estimation of independently existing properties, but a determination of certain magnitudes before the background of a number of accepted theories. *binary star: 쌍성

Based on discoveries in neuroscience, pain and pleasure are formed and processed in the same area of the brain. Our bodies constantly strive for homeostasis, which is defined as the balance of bodily functions. Without the body’s effective compensatory mechanisms, which may cushion potential highs and lows, we would not be capable of surviving. Pleasure and pain are like two sides of the same coin; they seem to work together and are heavily reliant on one another and keep balance. If you imagine pleasure and pain as the two opposite points on a scale, you can easily understand that as one of the two points rises, the other must correspondingly fall. We’ve all heard the expression, “No pain, no gain.” Well, according to psychiatrist Dr. Anna Lembke, there may be some truth to these words. She says that our attempts to escape being miserable are in fact making us even more miserable. This is because pain is actually an essential component of our ability to maintain a neutral state, and allowing it will in turn reset our internal scale back to balance.

(A), (B), (C)의 각 네모 안에서 문맥에 맞는 낱말로 가장적절한 것은?

문제 본문 다시 보기
Conditioned Place Preference is a way of finding out what animals want. Researchers train them to associate one place with an experience such as food or a loud noise and another place with something completely different, usually where nothing happens. The two places are made obviously different to make it as easy as possible for the animal to associate each place with what happened to it there. The animal's preference for being in one place or another is measured both before and after its experiences in the two places. If the animal starts spending more time in the place associated with the experience, this suggests that it (A) [disliked / enjoyed] the experience. Conversely, if it now avoids the place the stimulus appeared and starts to prefer the place it did not experience it, then this suggests that it found the stimulus (B) [pleasurable / uncomfortable]. For example, mice with cancer show a preference for the place where they have been given morphine, a drug used to relieve pain, rather than where they have received saline whereas healthy mice developed no such preference. This suggests that the mice with cancer (C) [wanted / avoided] the morphine.
정답: ②

보기와 우리말 뜻

enjoyed - uncomfortable - avoided → (A) enjoyed=즐겼다(enjoy=즐기다) / (B) uncomfortable=불편한 / (C) avoided=피했다(avoid=피하다)

enjoyed - uncomfortable - wanted → (A) enjoyed=즐겼다(enjoy=즐기다) / (B) uncomfortable=불편한 / (C) wanted=원했다(want=원하다)

enjoyed - pleasurable - avoided → (A) enjoyed=즐겼다(enjoy=즐기다) / (B) pleasurable=즐거운 / (C) avoided=피했다(avoid=피하다)

disliked - uncomfortable - avoided → (A) disliked=싫어했다(dislike=싫어하다) / (B) uncomfortable=불편한 / (C) avoided=피했다(avoid=피하다)

disliked - pleasurable - wanted → (A) disliked=싫어했다(dislike=싫어하다) / (B) pleasurable=즐거운 / (C) wanted=원했다(want=원하다)

Researchers train them to associate one place with an experience such as food or a loud noise and another place with something completely different, usually where nothing happens. If there is a shift in where the animal chooses to spend its time for the reward, this suggests that it liked the experience and is trying to repeat it. For example, mice with cancer show a preference for the place where they have been given morphine, a drug used to relieve pain, rather than where they have received saline whereas healthy mice developed no such preference. 다음 글의 밑줄 친 ⓐfamily resemblances가 의미하는 바로 알맞은 것은?

문제 본문 다시 보기
Some people argue that there is a single, logically consistent concept known as reading that can be neatly set apart from everything else people do with books. Is reading really that simple? The most productive way to think about reading is as a loosely related set of behaviors that belong together owing to ⓐfamily resemblances, as Ludwig Wittgenstein used the phrase, without having in common a single defining trait. Consequently, efforts to distinguish reading from nonreading are destined to fail because there is no agreement on what qualifies as reading in the first place. The more one tries to figure out where the border lies between reading and not-reading, the more edge cases will be found to stretch the term’s flexible boundaries. Thus, it is worth attempting to collect together these exceptional forms of reading into a single forum, one highlighting the challenges faced by anyone wishing to establish the boundaries where reading begins and ends. The attempt moves toward an understanding of reading as a spectrum that is expansive enough to accommodate the distinct reading activities.

a concept that things have overlapping similarities, whose border is vague (여러 것이 서로 겹치는 닮은 점을 가지지만 경계는 모호하다는 개념)

an idea that the family have one or more internal features in common (가족이 하나 이상의 내면적 특징을 공통으로 가진다는 생각)

쉬운 풀이

family resemblances(가족 유사성)는 가족끼리 누구는 눈이, 누구는 코가 닮듯이 서로 겹치는 닮은 점은 있지만 모두가 공유하는 하나의 특징은 없는 것을 말해요. 본문은 읽기 행동들이 '느슨하게 관련'되어 있고 '하나의 결정적인 특징이 없으며', 경계를 정하려 할수록 애매한 경우가 많아진다고 해요. 그래서 '겹치는 닮은 점이 있지만 경계가 모호한 개념'인 ①이 정답이에요.

다른 보기는 왜 아닐까요?

② 실제 가족의 신체적 닮음을 말하는 게 아니라 비유적인 표현이에요.

③ '하나의 결정적인 특징을 공유하지 않는다'고 했으므로 공통 특징이 있다는 말은 반대예요.

④ 읽기 행동들은 서로 느슨하게 관련(겹치는 닮은 점)되어 있으므로 반대예요.

⑤ 경계는 분명하지 않고 유연하다(flexible)고 했으므로 반대예요.
